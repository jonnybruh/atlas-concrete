Page Mill Residence.

Three stepped cedar board-form retaining walls on a steep Palo Alto lot, poured in two staged lifts to hold back a terraced garden and anchor a new foundation above.

01 The Problem

The lot drops eighteen feet across its width. The architect wanted three terraces — each anchored by a cast-in-place wall — with a single continuous board-form grain running across all of them. No obvious seams. No mismatched texture between lifts.

The challenge wasn't structural. The engineering was straightforward: #5 bar at 12" OC each face, 24" footings, drainage mat behind. The challenge was the face. Cedar board-form pulls its grain from whatever boards you use. If the lifts don't line up, you can read it across the wall, and the whole composition falls apart.

The approach

We milled all the form boards in one batch before the first pour — 1x6 rough cedar, numbered and stacked in sequence. The same boards were reused for each lift, in the same order, so the grain walked across the stepped walls as a single pattern. Tie holes were landed on a one-foot grid so the stripped face reads as a planned composition, not an accident of formwork.

The pour

Two lifts per wall, four feet maximum per lift, 48 hours between. Mix was 5,000 psi with a pozz blend and a mid-range plasticizer to get a clean consolidation without a lot of vibration — over-vibrating board-form concrete washes the grain out. Crew of six per pour day, in at 5:30, finished by noon.

The result

Fifteen yards, fourteen days, no callbacks. The architect signed off on the first pour without a single sack rub — a good day for the form crew. The terraces read as three lifts of a single wall, which is what was asked for.
