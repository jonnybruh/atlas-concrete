Bearing, shear, retaining.

Cast-in-place walls are our favorite thing to build. We own plywood, steel-ply, and board-form systems in-house — no subbed-out formwork, no mystery about who set the ties. When you hire us for a wall, the crew who builds the forms is the crew who strips them.

Bid this scope

Scope

Bearing walls, shear walls, retaining walls, basement walls, tilt-up panels (poured in place), architectural feature walls. Single-lift pours up to 12 feet; staged pours for anything taller. Board-form, plywood, or steel-ply systems.

Form systems we own

Steel-ply for production pours where rate and schedule matter. Plywood for flexibility and one-off shapes. Board-form for architectural walls where the face is the point. Our lead carpenter has twenty-two years tying up forms; we don't send anyone out green.

Mockups

Any architectural wall gets a mockup panel before production. The architect signs off on the board arrangement, tie pattern, chamfer detail, and sack rub intensity before we pour a single yard of the real wall. Cheap insurance.

02Wall types

Type 01 /

Shear walls

Cast-in-place lateral systems coordinated with steel embeds, hold-downs, and drag lines. We read the lateral drawings and place the bar ourselves.

Type 02 /

Bearing walls

Load-bearing walls for wood-frame or steel-framed structures above. Top-of-wall anchorage coordinated with framer.

Type 03 /

Retaining walls

Gravity, cantilever, and buttressed systems. Drainage, weep, and waterproofing coordinated in-house.

Type 04 /

Basement walls

Below-grade walls with waterproofing handoff, damp-proofing, drain mat, and keyway to the footing.

Type 05 /

Feature / architectural

Walls where the face is the point. Mockup-driven, carefully tied, poured slow, finished clean.

Type 06 /

Tilt-up (poured-in-place)

Not for us. We're a cast-in-place shop — if you're looking for tilt-up, we can point you to someone who does it well.
