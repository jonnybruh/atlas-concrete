Atlas About
Est. 1998 · Oakland

A form shop that grew up, kept its hands rough.

Atlas Concrete Works started as two guys, one pickup, and a trailer full of plywood. Twenty-eight years later, the trucks are bigger and the crews are four, but the only reason anyone hires us is still the same: we build the wall ourselves, and we don't leave until it's right.

01 The Story

Tom Merced and Danny Oberg met on a foundation crew in San Leandro in 1994. Four years later, they bought a used form truck, printed business cards at Kinko's, and poured their first job — a 28-yard garage slab off Mountain Boulevard — on a Saturday in October.

The first decade was all footings and stem walls. Residential remodels, hillside underpinning, the odd commercial TI. The work that nobody wants to bid because it's awkward, steep, or crowded — we took it, because that's what was available, and because getting a truck into a weird spot turns out to be a transferable skill.

By 2010 we had two crews and a yard. By 2018 we had four, a lead carpenter who'd been with us fourteen years, and a pour calendar that ran six weeks out. The work got bigger; the standards didn't budge. We still do our own formwork. We still meet you on-site. We still show up at 5:30 on pour day.

What hasn't changed

We bid flat. We pour clean. We stand behind the face of the wall. If the architect doesn't like the board-form pattern after strip, we've got a mockup panel we can point to — or we tear it out. That's the deal, and it hasn't moved since 1998.

02 How we work

Value 01 /

Own the form

A cast-in-place wall is only as good as its formwork. We build ours in-house, tie it ourselves, and pour it ourselves. The crew who sets the forms is the crew who strips them.

Value 02 /

Bid it flat, say it once

Flat-rate bids, broken down by scope. We don't nickel-and-dime changes; we re-bid them cleanly. If we quoted it wrong, that's on us, not a change order.

Value 03 /

Show up at 5:30

Pour day starts before sunrise so the concrete lands at the right temperature and the crew isn't rushed at finish. The truck rolls on time; every time.

Value 04 /

Mockup before production

Any architectural finish gets a mockup panel before the real pour. Cheaper than tearing out 80 cy because the sack rub looked different on the drawing.

Value 05 /

Leave it clean

Ties pulled, patches done, site swept, spoils hauled. The next trade should find a ready surface, not our trash.

Value 06 /

Stand behind the face

If the wall comes out wrong, we come back. Honor work doesn't go on a change order. It goes on our time.

03 Quality is the priority

Every wall we pour is a thing we'll drive past for the next thirty years. That's the standard.

Quality, for us, is not a marketing word — it's what we're doing with our hands every day. It shows up in the details you only see after strip: plumb corners, tight joints, clean chamfers, a sack rub you can read across a full panel.

Every lead on our crews has more than a decade behind them. Every pour is logged, every cylinder tracked, every mix verified against the structural spec. If a batch comes in wrong at the slump test, we send it back. That's happened twice in three years — and twice is twice too many.

When something does go sideways, we own it. No second contractor, no letter from a lawyer. You call Tom or Danny. That part hasn't changed since the Kinko's business cards.
